The core question

The ICP is defined by a number no database contains. What do you filter on instead?

Problem
The ideal customer profile was defined by personal taxable income over $600,000. No B2B database carries that field, and it never will, because it is not public. Industry was "most of them", and three overlapping size and revenue brackets were ticked, which narrowed nothing.
System
Replaced the unsourceable attribute with a sourceable proxy, a licensed profession that reliably clears the threshold, is the right kind of business owner for the offer, and is enumerable by state from public directories rather than bought from a vendor.
Output
The proxy converted at roughly twice the rate of the literal brief, measured against 65,587 sends of a deliberately retained control that ran the ICP exactly as written.

The client

A tax advisory and tax-planning practice in California. The offer, in their own words on the onboarding form: custom strategies to reduce a business owner's taxes by anywhere from $30,000 to $150,000+, depending on how much they make. The target was 40 leads a month.

The problem: an ICP nobody sells

The ideal customer profile, as written on the form:

Business owners in the United States, but in California, whose taxable income is over $600,000.

That is a perfectly sensible commercial definition and a completely unusable targeting one. Personal taxable income is not a field in any B2B database. Vendors sell headcount, company revenue, industry, technographics and funding events. Nobody sells this person personally cleared $600K last year, and nobody ever will, because it is not public.

Everything else pointed the same way. Industry: "I don't have a specific industry. We work with most of the industries." Company size: three brackets ticked, 1&ndash;10 through 50&ndash;100. Estimated customer revenue: three brackets ticked, $0&ndash;100K through $500K&ndash;1M, which narrows nothing and, read literally, describes companies too small to generate a $600K personal income.

Most of the remaining answers were deferrals. Five ideal customers: "I can show you my client profile." Competitors: "I can give you a list." Intent signals: "I don't know." Directories of the ICP: "I'm not sure what this means."

None of that is a criticism of the client. It is the normal state of an onboarding form, and it is exactly why targeting has to be derived rather than transcribed. A form that answers every question is rare. A form that answers none of the ones that matter for list-building is the job.

Two things it did give, and both mattered: geography, and exclusions, no accounting, no tax, no non-profit, because they will not prospect their own industry.

What I did: proxy the income with a profession

If you cannot filter on income, filter on something that implies it and that you can actually source.

The proxy was attorneys, partners and firm owners in one state. The logic is three-part, and every part has to hold or the proxy is worthless:

They clear the threshold. A partner or owner at a firm in that state is a plausible $600K+ earner far more often than a randomly drawn "business owner".

They are the right kind of business owner. The pitch is entity structure and tax strategy for someone with pass-through income. A law-firm principal is precisely that buyer.

They are listed publicly, by profession, with geography attached, in legal directories and the state bar roll, rather than sold by a data vendor. The population is enumerable without buying it, and enumerable by the exact filter that matters.

That third point is the whole trick. Switching the ICP from an unsourceable attribute to a sourceable one turns an impossible list into a directory scrape.

The account also kept an all-industries master campaign as a control, the literal reading of the brief, business owners across any industry, no professional proxy. That control is what makes the next section a measurement rather than an assertion.

The proxy converted at roughly twice the rate of the literal brief, across 65,587 sends of control volume. A smaller multiple than the list-source result on the investment-bank account, and a real one measured over a large sample.

The best individual campaigns are all profession-sourced, 0.150%, 0.145% and 0.128% on separate splits. The all-industries campaigns cluster between 0.016% and 0.037%, apart from one early outlier.

The caveat that travels with the reply rate

87 of the 92 campaigns in this workspace count auto-replies in their statistics. That means the 1.43% reply rate here is inflated by out-of-office traffic by an unknown amount.

The interested figure is unaffected, because it is a flag a human sets in the master inbox after reading the reply. That is exactly why every comparison above is ranked on positives and not on replies.

What I would do differently

The auto-reply setting should have been corrected here, not just diagnosed elsewhere. It is still on in 87 campaigns, which means eleven months of reply-rate history in the largest account of its kind is not comparable to anything.

The control was never re-run. The all-industries campaigns date from early in the engagement while the profession lists kept being iterated. A proxy-versus-literal comparison is strongest when both arms run in the same window against the same copy. Here they did not.

The proxy was never widened. Attorneys are one profession that clears $600K. Physicians, dentists, specialist practice owners and independent financial advisors are others, all with the same directory-sourceable property. The account ran one profession for eleven months, which leaves the central hypothesis supported by exactly one instance of it.

No booking system existed at onboarding. The form recorded no calendar account at all. 144 calls have since been booked, so it was solved during the engagement, but a cold campaign pointing at a calendar that does not exist yet is a preventable first-week problem.

The transferable lesson

When the ICP is defined by an attribute nobody sells, find a proxy that is both correlated with it and enumerable, then run the literal brief as the control so you can prove the proxy was worth it.

Half of that is the idea. The other half is the control campaign, and it is the half people skip. Without those 65,587 sends against the brief as written, "we targeted this profession because they earn more" is a story. With them, it is 0.080% against 0.038%.

What they kept

  • A buildable list for an ICP that could not be bought
  • A control arm that turns the claim into a measurement
  • A sourcing pattern that transfers to any licensed profession
  • The auto-reply defect documented against eleven months of history
