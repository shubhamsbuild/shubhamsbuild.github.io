For Raw Canvas · launch films

Find the launch before it's announced.

Big launch films don't come from 300 emails. They come from a handful of companies a week, each about to have a moment it can't hide, reached before they start shopping for an agency.

THE RULETwo signals per lead, and one of them must have a date.
The idea

Companies can hide a launch. They can't hide the paperwork.

Before launching, a company has to file and register things in public. Those records show up months before the announcement, while there's still time to make a film.

The signals

Where launches leave a trail, and how to read it.

Four places to look. Each signal says where to find it, how to use it, and what to watch out for. No signal is enough on its own: pair one that shows money or a moment with one that shows a date.

★ = strongest signals · right column = how far ahead of launch it usually appears

1. The paper trail

Public records a company has to file before it launches. Hard to fake, and almost no agency watches them.

★Form D filingThey raised money and haven't announced it yet.1–2 months
Where
SEC EDGAR, the US public filings database. New Form Ds appear daily; filter by form type "D".
What's in it
Company name and city, amount raised, date of the first investment, industry, and the names of the executives and directors.
How to use
Keep tech companies raising roughly $5–50M, check the raise hasn't been announced, then read the directors list: a new VC partner on the board tells you which fund led, and whether Raw Canvas can get a warm intro.
Watch out
US companies only. Not every startup files, and some file late. Investment funds and real estate deals file too, so filter them out.
Trademark filed as "intent to use"A new product name they plan to launch but haven't yet.3–6+ months
Where
USPTO trademark search (also EUIPO for Europe, and India's IP office).
How to use
"Intent to use" means the name isn't on the market yet. A new name from a funded startup usually means a new product or a rebrand on the way.
Watch out
Filed very early, and some names never launch. Always pair with a second signal.
★FCC equipment filingA device is being certified before it goes on sale.3–6 months
Where
The FCC ID database. Anything with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or cellular needs this certification before it's sold in the US.
How to use
Companies can ask the FCC to keep product photos and manuals confidential for a while (up to about 180 days with extensions). That window gives away roughly when the product launches.
Watch out
Hardware only. Big electronics companies file constantly, so focus on startups and direct-to-consumer brands.
New subdomains appearingA new site is being built before anything is linked publicly.2–8 weeks
Where
crt.sh, a free public log of website security certificates. Every new subdomain gets one.
How to use
Watch a shortlist of target companies for subdomains like launch., beta., v2. or a new product name.
Watch out
Noisy: internal tools and test sites show up too. Only useful on companies already on your list.
Moving to a better domainA rebrand is coming.1–3 months
Where
Domain registration records, new redirects, a new logo trademark.
How to use
A company buying the short version of its name (getflux.com → flux.com) is usually preparing a rebrand, and a rebrand needs a film to carry it.
Watch out
Sometimes it's just a domain purchase. Look for a second sign, like a new brand agency or a design hire.
FDA clearance (medtech)A medical device was just cleared for sale.1–4 months
Where
The FDA's public 510(k) clearance database.
How to use
Clearance is the green light for commercial launch. Medtech launches need films for clinicians, investors and buyers.
Watch out
Only relevant if Raw Canvas wants to work in health and medtech.

2. People who know first

Not data: relationships. These people see a launch weeks before anyone else, and each one can bring several a year.

★VC partners and platform teamsThey know their portfolio's launch calendar.2–4 months
Where
Raw Canvas's existing relationships with funds.
How to use
Ask each fund once a quarter which portfolio companies are launching or announcing next. Their intro arrives warm and early.
Watch out
Partners protect their founders. Make the intro easy and worth their while, and never spam their portfolio.
PR agenciesHired 1–3 months before a big announcement.1–3 months
Where
PR firms' "welcome our new client" posts, and direct relationships.
How to use
They plan the launch, and the launch needs a video. A referral swap works both ways: Raw Canvas sends them clients too.
Watch out
Some agencies have in-house video. Partner with the ones that don't.
Brand and design agenciesA rebrand is in progress.2–6 months
How to use
When an agency is building a new brand, the reveal needs a film. Same referral swap as with PR.
Watch out
Big brand studios often bring their own film partners. Smaller studios are the better fit.
Conference organisersThey know who has the keynote and launch slots.2–4 months
How to use
Companies launching on stage need a film for the moment. Organisers can introduce Raw Canvas or list it as a recommended partner.

3. Moves you can see

Public behaviour that shows a launch is coming. Easier to find, but more agencies notice it.

★First user conference announcedA fixed date, and a keynote that needs an opening film.2–5 months
Where
Event pages on Luma and Eventbrite, and "Summit" or "Conf" pages on company sites.
How to use
Pitch the opening film plus clips cut from the event, which plays to Raw Canvas's clipping work.
Watch out
Rarer than raises. Strongest when the company also raised recently.
New marketing leader, first 90 daysNew CMOs want a visible early win.2–6 months
Where
LinkedIn job changes for CMO, VP Marketing, Head of Brand.
How to use
A brand or launch film is the classic early win. Much stronger if they bought one at their last company.
Watch out
No date on its own. Needs a second signal.
Job post that names the launch"You'll lead go-to-market for our Q1 launch."2–5 months
Where
Company careers pages and job boards: product marketing, brand, founding marketer roles.
How to use
The post often gives the launch timing, and shows there's no video team yet.
Contract video producer listingThey want a video and have nobody to make it.2–6 weeks
Where
Job boards and freelance platforms.
How to use
Clear intent. Offer a team instead of a freelancer.
Watch out
Often late, and the budget may be freelancer-sized.
Founder teaser posts"Something big is coming."1–3 weeks
Where
Founders' posts on X and LinkedIn: countdowns, "18 months heads down".
How to use
Usually too late for the hero film, but right for launch-week cut-downs and clips.
Someone asking for an agency"Who made this video?" or a request for recommendations.2–6 weeks
Where
Posts, comments on well-known launch films, founder Slack communities, requests for proposals.
How to use
The highest intent there is: reply fast with one relevant film.
Watch out
They're already comparing quotes, so Raw Canvas competes on price instead of writing the brief.

4. Raw Canvas's own data

The warmest signals, because these people already know the work.

★A past client changes jobsSomeone who already bought from Raw Canvas joins a new company.any time
Where
LinkedIn, tracking every past client contact.
How to use
They've already said yes once. Reach out in their first months at the new company.
Made a launch film beforeThey already pay for this kind of work.any time
Where
The company's YouTube or Vimeo channel.
How to use
If the last film is 18+ months old, the next one is due. Wait for a dated signal, then pitch an upgrade.
Repeat visits to Raw Canvas's workThey keep coming back to the case studies.any time
Where
Website-visitor tracking on rawcanvas.co.
How to use
A short, direct note. They already know who Raw Canvas is.
Watch out
Check privacy rules before setting up visitor tracking.
The strongest plays

Three to start with.

1

The quiet raise best fit

A startup files a Form D but hasn't announced the raise. The filing names the VC partner who joined the board. If Raw Canvas knows that fund, ask the partner for an intro and pitch the announcement film.

2

Their own stage highest intent

A recently funded company announces its first user conference. The date is fixed and the keynote needs an opening film, plus clips from the event.

3

Hardware in the FCC window nobody watches it

Devices must be certified by the FCC before sale. Companies can ask to keep product photos secret for a few months, which gives away the launch window. Biggest budgets of all.

The offer

At this budget, they're not worried about price. They're worried about risk.

  • "It'll look generic."Script and storyboard approved first.
  • "It'll miss our launch."A committed delivery date.
  • "Nobody will watch it."Cut-downs and founder clips included.
  • "It'll eat my time."One shoot day. We handle the rest.

The first email doesn't ask for a call. It offers a one-page treatment written for their launch.

The copy

Short, specific, and a sample of the work.

Subject: the film for [Co]'s next chapter

[Name], if there's an announcement on the horizon for [Co], most go out as a blog post and a logo. Yours could be the one people share: [one-line concept].

We made [past client]'s launch film ([link], 40 sec).

I wrote a one-page treatment for yours. Want me to send it over?

Under 90 words. Never mention the filing or the signal: use it for timing, not as the opener.

To make it real

Four questions for Raw Canvas.

  1. What was happening at your last 10 clients, 6 weeks before they signed?
  2. What's your real production lead time?
  3. Which VCs and PR firms do you already know?
  4. What's your price range, and the film you're proudest of?
