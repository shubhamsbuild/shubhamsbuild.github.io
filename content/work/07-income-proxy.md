## Problem one: the ICP is a number nobody sells

*Business owners with personal taxable income over $600,000.* Correct about who
pays them. Useless as a list instruction.

**Personal income is not public.** No vendor carries it and none ever will. The
rest of the brief ruled almost nothing out either: industry was "most of them",
with three overlapping size brackets ticked.

**Solved by finding something that comes with it.** A licensed profession whose
members reliably clear that threshold, who are the right kind of owner for the
offer, and who every state publishes a register of. No vendor, no credits, no
argument about coverage.

## Problem two: which profession?

Not knowable from a desk. So nine verticals ran: dentists, marketing agencies,
SaaS, solar, restaurants, gyms, roofers, physicians, brokers.

**35,338 sends across those, 18 positive replies.** Attorneys were the one that
held, and the account rebuilt around state bar and attorney directories.

![A licensed-profession proxy converted at 0.080 percent against 0.038 percent for the brief as written](/assets/exhibit-8-proxy-vs-control.svg)

## The part I would want to be asked about

I kept the original brief running. A segment built on the ICP exactly as written
stayed live for **65,587 sends.**

That is the difference between an opinion and a result. Replacing a client's stated
definition of their own customer and simply asserting the new one is better is not
something you can defend when they ask. Keeping the old one alive makes it
measurable, and the answer was **2.1x**.

If it had gone the other way I would have known that too, which is the point.

## What it produced

**446,457 sends over eleven months. 313 positive replies, 270 of them from the
proxy. 153 meetings booked.**

Outcome is unrecorded: every booking row still reads *scheduled*, so the defensible
verb is *booked*, not *held*.
