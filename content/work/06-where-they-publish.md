## The account

The **largest and best-performing account in the book.** 777,724 sent is more than any other
workspace, and **2,582 interested is more than every other client account combined.**

At **0.332% positive** it runs roughly **four times the portfolio average.** It is the outlier,
and understanding why is worth more than any single technique here.

The market, read off the campaign construction: creators, coaches and agency owners, the creator economy and the information-product businesses around it.

## Four ways to build the same list

The account is effectively a natural experiment, because four genuinely different acquisition
methods ran side by side at volume against the same market.

**Platform creator directories.** Creators on the major short-form and image platforms,
segmented by category: business, finance, career, e-commerce.

**Social-graph scraping.** Followers of specific large accounts, verified and cleaned, plus a
scraper producing master lists off a text-first platform.

**Tool-user targeting.** Users of the funnel and course platforms this market runs on. The
reasoning is good on its face: if someone runs a funnel tool, they sell information products.

**Job-title targeting.** Coaches and professional-training businesses pulled from a B2B
database by title, and a straight agency-owner list.

## The numbers

**777,724 sent, 11,683 unique replies at 1.50%, 2,582 interested at 0.332%, bounce 0.76%.
145 calls booked.**

| List construction | Camps | Sent | uReply | Interested | Pos% |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **Creator platforms and follower scrapes** | 28 | 207,715 | 2,878 | **862** | **0.415%** |
| Coaches and training, by title | 14 | 175,422 | 3,048 | 503 | 0.287% |
| Tool users | 13 | 203,528 | 2,698 | 464 | 0.228% |
| **Agency owners, by title** | 1 | 38,663 | 155 | **6** | **0.016%** |
| Everything else | 39 | 152,396 | 2,904 | 747 | 0.490% |

**The spread between the best and worst construction is roughly twenty-six times**, creator lists at 0.415% against the agency-owner list at 0.016%, on comparable volume.

Two campaigns carry the thesis. A short-form creator list segmented by category: **19,913 sent,
211 interested, 1.060%**, the single best campaign result anywhere in this portfolio,
twelve times the portfolio average. An image-platform creator list across multiple categories:
**40,374 sent, 272 interested, 0.674%.**

And the counter-example, at scale: an agency-owner campaign sent **38,663 emails for 6
interested.** Same account, same infrastructure, same window.

## What this proves

**Targeting the platform someone sells *on* beat targeting the job title they *hold*, by a wide
margin.** A creator listed in a finance category is definitionally in the market. Someone whose
database title reads "agency owner" may be a solo freelancer or a services firm with no
interest in the offer at all.

That is the same finding as the investment-bank account, the list source outperformed
everything else that could be varied, arrived at independently, on a completely
different market, with a twenty-six-fold spread instead of a nine-fold one.

The tool-user hypothesis is the interesting near-miss. Targeting funnel and course-platform
users is a *clever* proxy, and at 0.228% it beat job titles comfortably. It still lost to
simply going where the audience publishes, by nearly two to one. **Proximity to the buying
signal beat cleverness about it.**

## The caveat that travels with the reply rate

**84 of the 95 campaigns count auto-replies in their statistics**, so the 1.50% reply rate here
is inflated by out-of-office traffic by an unknown amount. The interested figure is unaffected,
since it is a human flag set during inbox triage. Every comparison above is ranked on positives
for that reason.

## What I would do differently

**The agency-owner arm ran to 38,663 sends before stopping.** At the rate it was returning,
that was knowable by ten thousand. There was no kill threshold.

**The creator finding was never pushed.** Creator lists were 28 of 95 campaigns and produced
the best rates on the account by a wide margin. Volume kept going into constructions that
measurably converted worse.

**The auto-reply setting is on in 84 campaigns**, which makes a year of reply-rate history in
the largest account here non-comparable.

**No written brief exists for this account.** Target, constraints, and what was actually asked
for are not on record anywhere. The account that most deserves a documented brief is the one
that has none.

## The transferable lesson

**Target where the buyer publishes, not what a database calls them.** A job title is a third
party's guess about someone's role. A creator profile in a category is that person telling you
what they do, in public, on the platform they make money on.

And the uncomfortable one: **this is the best account in the portfolio and it has the least
documentation.** Results and record-keeping turn out to be independent variables, and only one
of them survives the engagement.
