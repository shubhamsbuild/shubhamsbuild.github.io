## Why this account books

The four-way test below is the reason. Ranking the list constructions and then
working the best one first is what turns 2,582 positive replies into **143 booked
meetings**, and this is the one account in the whole portfolio where the calendar
actually recorded what happened next: **112 held, one no-show.**

Everywhere else, meetings sit as *scheduled* forever and the honest verb stops at
*booked*. Here it does not.

## The problem

The client sells to online creators, coaches and course sellers. There are at least
four sensible ways to build a list of those people, and the account was already
running all four:

the directories of the platforms they publish on, the followers of accounts in that
world, the software that kind of business runs on, and the plain approach, ask a
B2B database for everyone whose job title says "coach".

Four methods, all live, and **nobody had ever compared them.** So the team was busy
improving the wording inside whichever list happened to be in front of them, with no
idea whether the list itself was the thing holding it back.

## How I approached it

Treat the list construction as the thing being tested, and hold everything else
still, same market, same inboxes, same sending window. Then rank the four on one
number.

Choosing that number mattered more than it sounds. The obvious choice is reply rate,
and it would have been wrong: **84 of the 95 campaigns count automatic replies in
their statistics.** Out-of-office bounces would have been counted as interest, and
the construction that generated the most noise would have looked like the winner.

So I ranked on positive replies per person contacted instead, people who actually
said yes, divided by people we actually wrote to.

## What I found

Where somebody publishes beats what a database calls them, by about twenty-six
times on comparable volume. 0.415% against 0.016%.

It makes sense once you see it. A job title is a label someone else assigned, often
years ago, often wrong. A listing on a creator platform is a decision that person
made about where they sell, current, deliberate, and far closer to the question of
whether they will buy.

The best single campaign in the whole portfolio came out of this account at 1.060%,
roughly twelve times the average across every client.

## The catch

Platform directories are finite. You can exhaust one, and then it is gone, so the
ranking is not an argument for deleting the other three constructions. It is an
argument for **order**: work the best one until it runs dry, then move down the
list. Knowing the order is the whole value.
