## Problem one: four list theories, none of them tested

The client sells to online creators, coaches and course sellers. There are four
sensible ways to build that list, and the account was already running all four:
the platforms they publish on, the people they follow, the funnel software they
run on, and asking a database for everyone whose title says "coach".

**Nobody had ranked them.** So effort went into rewriting copy inside whichever
list happened to be in front of someone, with no idea whether the list was the
thing holding it back.

## Problem two: the obvious metric would have picked the wrong winner

Reply rate is the instinct, and it is wrong here. **Most of these campaigns count
automatic replies in their statistics**, so out-of-office volume scores as
interest and the noisiest construction looks like the best one.

**Solved by ranking on positive replies per person contacted.** People who
actually said yes, over people actually written to.

![Creator-platform lists converted at 2.37 percent per contact against 0.63 percent for follower scrapes](/assets/exhibit-9-list-constructions.svg)

## Where somebody publishes beats what a database calls them

A job title is a label someone else assigned, often years ago, often wrong. A
listing on a creator platform is a decision that person made about where they
sell: current, deliberate, and far closer to whether they will buy.

**Then doubled down.** Creator platforms went from one test to twenty-four
campaigns and 43,693 contacts, and produced 1,034 of the account's 2,582 positive
replies.

## The catch

Platform directories are finite. Exhaust one and it is gone, so this is not an
argument for deleting the other three constructions. It is an argument for
**order**: work the best one until it runs dry, then move down. Knowing the order
is the whole value.

This is also the only account in the portfolio where the calendar recorded what
happened next, so *held* is sayable here and nowhere else.
