## The problem

By this point another account had produced a clear result: lists built from what
people *did* beat lists built from what their job title says. The obvious move is
to apply that everywhere.

This account is where that stops being true, and I would rather find that out on
purpose than have an interviewer find it for me.

## How I approached it

Run both in the same account, in the same weeks, and compare.

The customer here is enterprise sales reps at software companies. I pulled a sample
of the audience and read it: the job titles came back almost identical, over and
over. That is what a correct filter looks like. For this product, the title really
is the qualifier. Somebody's job *is* the reason they need it.

So the clever approach had nothing to add, and the plain one was not laziness. It
was right.

## The part that did work

Alongside the filtered lists I ran two small ones built on a **story about the
person** instead of their job title. Sales people whose background suggests they
would make good founders. Former professional athletes who moved into enterprise
sales.

Neither of those is a field in any database. "Used to play professionally, now sells
software" exists only in the words someone wrote about themselves, so the list has
to be assembled by reading profiles. That is why they are 56 and 36 people rather
than 200.

Per person contacted, they were the strongest thing in the account.

## What it did

**13 meetings in four days**, and the highest LinkedIn acceptance rate of any
account I run, 48%, against 10% on another.

And the plain filtered lists returned 21 interested from 782, a better return per
send than behaviour-based sourcing managed elsewhere.

## The catch

Story-based lists do not scale, and the mistake is trying.

Take something that got 7 replies from 56 people and rebuild it at 500, and the
story has to loosen until it means roughly "works in sales". At that point the
opening line is no longer true of the person reading it, and they can tell. The
rule I use is that if a list cannot be built at the size you want without weakening
the detail that defines it, it is finished. Go and find a different story rather
than a bigger version of this one.
