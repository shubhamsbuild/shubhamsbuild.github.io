## The problem

The customer is a medical practice. You can buy a list of physicians easily. Every data vendor sells one, and so does every competitor pitching the same
practices.

The trouble is that a list of physicians tells you who *could* buy, not who is
ready to. A practice that has run the same way for six years and a practice that
changed something last month look identical on that list. Only one of them has a
reason to take the call.

## How I approached it

I stopped asking *who is the customer* and started asking **what has to happen at a
practice before they need this.**

Those are events, not attributes. A practice opens. It takes on another doctor. It
hires someone to chase up patients who stopped coming in. A doctor finishes
training and is about to sign a contract. A practice stops accepting Medicare and
starts charging patients directly.

Every one of those changes how the practice runs, every one creates a problem this
product solves, and none of them is a box you can tick on a data vendor's website.
They have to be found.

## What I built

A separate campaign for each event, labelled so anyone can tell them apart, and each one built twice over.

That second part is the piece that does the work. One version goes to people we are
not connected to, and asks to connect. The other goes to people already in the
network, as a direct message. Same trigger, completely different opening line,
because "we're connected and I noticed this" and "we have never spoken and I
noticed this" are not the same sentence and should not be written as if they were.

Every campaign carries its sender and its date, so a stale one is visible rather
than quietly running on a reason that has expired.

Before writing any of this down, I pulled a sample of the people actually in those
campaigns and read their profiles. The names on the campaigns claimed one audience;
I wanted to see whether the campaigns contained it. They did: physicians and
clinical leadership, matching what each trigger said it was looking for.

## What it did

**32 meetings in 11 days.** 503 LinkedIn invitations, and no email at all.

Fifteen of those landed in the final week, so it was still accelerating when I
pulled the numbers. The lesson is not that LinkedIn beats email. It is that
knowing *why* you are writing this week beats sending more.
