## Problem one: "physicians" is not a moment

Any vendor will sell a list of physicians. So will every competitor pitching the
same practices. It tells you who *could* buy, not who is ready to.

A practice that has run the same way for six years and one that changed something
last month look identical on that list.

**Solved by keying on change, not attributes.** A practice opens. It takes on
another doctor. It hires someone to chase up patients who stopped coming in. A
doctor finishes training and is about to sign a contract. A practice stops taking
Medicare and starts charging patients directly. Each is a campaign with a letter,
a sender and a date in its name.

## Problem two: the same trigger needs two different openers

"We're connected and I noticed this" and "we have never spoken and I noticed this"
are not the same sentence, and sending the first to a stranger is how a good
trigger gets wasted.

**Solved by building every trigger twice:** one version requests a connection,
one messages people already in the network.

![Messaging existing connections produced four interested replies from fifteen conversations while 168 new connection requests produced none](/assets/exhibit-11-eureka-routing.svg)

In the first fortnight that split was the whole difference. The connection
requests were still sitting unaccepted; the conversations with people already in
the network were already running.

## What it produced

**32 meetings in 11 days, from 503 invitations and no email at all.** Fifteen
landed in the final week, so it was still accelerating when the numbers were
pulled.

The campaign names here are unusually descriptive, which is exactly when they are
worth checking rather than trusting. A sample of the live audience returned
physicians and clinical leadership matching what each trigger claimed.
