01 The read

A media partner for the people who move markets, sold to very few of them.

What

Podcast production, launch and tech videos, virality and clipping This runs through four stages: Audit, Architect, Produce, Amplify. The client "just shows up and talks."

VC funds and scaling tech companies The site names Greycroft, Gainsight, Cluely, Not Another CEO and Seth Farbman.

How

"We work with a small number of clients" Deals are few and high in value, and they close on a call. That makes this a relationship business, not a volume business, so every play here sends to ten people, not ten thousand.

02 Who to go after

Three buyers. All three are assumptions for you to correct.

Assumption A

The VC fund with something to say

$100M to $3B AUM, with a platform or content hire, and a partner who already speaks in public. They're raising a new fund or have just closed one.

Buyer: Head of Platform, Chief of Staff, or the media-minded partner. Looks like your client Greycroft, whose Chief of Staff gave you a testimonial.

Assumption B

The tech company with a media moment

Seed to Series C, having just raised or with a launch coming up. Two or three months of content to build and nobody in-house to make it.

Buyer: founder, CMO or Head of Brand. Looks like your clients Cluely and Gainsight.

Assumption C

The operator who speaks but isn't clipped

A founder or CEO who's a regular on stages and podcasts, where every talk lives once on someone else's channel and then disappears.

Buyer: the person themselves. Looks like your client David Politis of Not Another CEO.

What I'd ask you to confirm

Which of the three closes fastest? What's a typical first engagement worth? Which logos will take a reference call? The answers change which workflow runs first.

03 The rule every play follows

For a media company, the outreach should be the media.

Everyone in this market sends "we help funds build their media." You can send three clips cut from the prospect's own talk before they've agreed to anything. It costs about an hour of editor time per lead, which is exactly why it only goes to ten people.

Volume

10 a week, not 1,000The constraint is editor hours, and that's what keeps the list honest.

Signal

Something they just did in publicA talk, a raise, a launch, a podcast gone quiet. Never just a job title.

Channel

Email, LinkedIn, then something physical for the top threeRelationships first, reply rate second.

Workflow 4 Relationships, not sequences

Don't pitch your best accounts. Put them on a show.

You already book guests for clients. Turn that around: run a Raw Canvas show (working title "Market Movers") where every guest is a target account. The invite is the outreach, and the episode is the relationship.

Invite

Twenty top-tier ICP accounts a quarter. Nobody declines being asked onto a show.

Record

A 40-minute conversation. That's one hour of their time and a real relationship.

Deliver

Their episode plus five clips, cut to their own channel's format. They experience the full service for free.

Amplify

They post it, their network sees your work, and your next guest is introduced by this one.

The in-person version

A quarterly recording dinner in NYC or SF with eight platform heads from funds, plus one live mic. Each guest leaves with a clip of themselves by the next morning. It's the relationship play he asked about, but it still produces content.

Offline Three plays that leave the inbox

Low volume, high salience. Each one carries their own words back to them.

Play 1 · cake or donut drop

Their best moment, on a cake

An edible print of a still from their talk, with the quote on the box and a QR code to the three clips. It goes to the office, not their home, two days after the clip email.

Who: the top 3 of the 10. Why it works: it gets photographed and posted, and the post is your ad.

Play 2 · Handwrytten letter

A real letter with one transcript line

A pen-plotted letter that quotes the 14-second line from their session you'd lead with, plus a QR code. Order it by hand from Handwrytten now, and wire up the API only once volume justifies it.

Guardrail: it's a real letter, never framed as "I wrote this by hand." That's where the trick turns into a trust problem.

Play 3 · the room drop

Be in the speaker's room before they're on stage

At an upcoming event, leave a card at the speaker's hotel or green room: "Your session's at 2:40. Clips in your inbox by 6." Then deliver on it.

Pattern: Brex's CFO hotel-room drops instead of a booth. A gift does the most when the buyer rarely gets one.

Sources: Sam Blond on guerrilla GTM (via Troy Kirwin), and the "works, but leave it" verdict on simulated handwriting. Both are from my own GTM field guide.
